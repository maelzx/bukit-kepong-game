# Historical note and list of gameplay abstractions

## The event

On **23 February 1950**, during the Malayan Emergency, the police station at
**Bukit Kepong**, in the Muar district of Johor, was attacked before dawn by
communist insurgents. A small number of police personnel — together with
family members and auxiliaries who were at the station — resisted the assault.
The station was overrun and burnt, and many of its defenders were killed. The
events are commemorated in Malaysia as an act of duty and resistance.

## What this game is

This game is a **dramatised interactive interpretation**. It is not a
documentary reconstruction, and it should not be used as a source of historical
fact. Where the historical record is uncertain or unavailable, the game makes
choices that serve playability.

## Everything below is invented for gameplay

These are abstractions, not history:

- **The map.** The compound dimensions, the four gates, the positions of the
  quarters, cookhouse, store shed, wire perimeter, sandbag emplacements, tracks
  and treeline are all invented. The river along the western edge stands in for
  the Muar River but its position and shape on the map are not a survey.
- **The player character.** A generic police officer. Not a portrayal of any
  specific person who was present.
- **The constables.** Six defenders holding fixed posts. The names shown
  (Jamil, Hassan, Othman, Mohd Yusof, Abu Bakar, Ibrahim) are common Malay
  names used as placeholders. They are **not** the names of the real people who
  were at Bukit Kepong.
- **The attackers.** Represented in three gameplay roles — rifleman, rusher and
  marksman. Real attacking forces were not organised into these categories.
- **Numbers, waves and the clock.** The eight-wave structure and the five-minute
  survival timer are game design. The size of the attacking force and the
  duration of the real attack are not modelled.
- **Fire as a mechanic.** Attackers setting the building alight, a numeric
  "structural integrity" value, and the player beating out flames with a bucket
  are all game systems. They are not a reconstruction of how the real station
  came to be burnt.
- **The victory condition.** "Survive the assault" is a game objective. It does
  **not** represent the historical outcome, in which the station fell.
- **All statistics.** Health, weapon damage, rates of fire, magazine sizes,
  reload times, movement speeds and the station's "structural integrity" bar
  are balance numbers, not measurements.

## What the game tries to keep period-appropriate

- Weapons are limited to those plausible for the setting: a Sten Mk II
  submachine gun and .303 bolt-action rifles for the police, mixed rifles and
  submachine guns for the attackers. No modern weapons, vehicles or equipment
  appear.
- Police are shown in khaki with the songkok.
- The opposing force is referred to throughout as **communist insurgents /
  communist guerrillas**.
- The setting is 1950s rural Johor: kampung buildings on stilts with hipped
  attap and plank roofs, oil lamps, water jars, a bicycle, sampan on the river,
  rubber and jungle trees, banana and coconut palms.

For the actual history, consult published historical sources rather than this
game.
